combinationTwo dials together: an actual oil painting

Oil ↔ pixel at −3 on its own gives a photoreal period drama. Add a datamosh row at +1.5 or +2.25 and the scene becomes a painted canvas with visible brushwork, on both seeds. A meaningless placebo dial pushed just as hard doesn't do it, and a generic “photoreal ↔ stylized” direction only gets a half-painted mural. Push two dials too far and the scene collapses instead.

styleEras, formats and a negative result

Medium words mostly arrive as an era, setting or format: silent film gives 1920s clothes and carriages, VHS gives a 4:3 frame and older trains. Describing the artifact instead (grain, dust, scratches) gave a cool grade and no grain, a negative result kept on purpose.

notesWhat these taught us about making dials

  • Pair agreement is a floor, not a verdict. Make Dial's agreement score was 0.75 to 0.85 for dials that worked and for dials that failed alike. Only renders tell them apart.
  • Style words arrive as an era first. Silent film, oil painting and VHS gave clothes, carriages and aspect ratios before (or instead of) the medium itself.
  • Check against a placebo. Any push the size of a dial reshuffles the background. What a meaningless dial of the same size doesn't do is what your dial does.
  • Put the subject in the scene. A creature or object continuum has nothing to act on if the prompt has none.
  • Two rows can do what neither does. Glitch + period drama made a painting; too much summed push breaks the scene.

One prompt and one or two seeds per finding, so treat these as starting points. Each dial file carries its own description, labels and useful/overcook strengths (open ⚙ on its row in the Dial Rack).
